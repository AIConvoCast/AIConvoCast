Today we will be looking at GPT 6 coming to everyday ChatGPT with interactive answers, Anthropic's new Claude Haiku 5.5 and its very low pricing, Microsoft and NVIDIA's plan to run AI agents locally on Windows PCs inside enforced limits, and a 1.8 billion dollar effort by the U.S. government, Google and Meta to build open biological data.

OpenAI is bringing GPT 6 to the main Chat tab in ChatGPT, and with it a new feature called Intelligent UI. Answers no longer have to be just text. They can include charts you can adjust, forms you can fill out, buttons, and small tools built for whatever you asked about. If you want a mortgage explained, you might get a working calculator inside the answer instead of a paragraph of math.

OpenAI describes the bigger goal this way: "Instead of people adapting to software, software will adapt to people." That's a lot more ambitious than adding visuals to answers. OpenAI is suggesting that the chat window could replace a lot of single-purpose apps by building the right tool when you need it. For developers, the real question is whether these generated controls hold up well enough to use every day, or whether they stay impressive demos.

Anthropic, meanwhile, released Claude Haiku 5.5, its smallest and fastest model, and priced it very aggressively. For prompts up to 100,000 tokens, it costs ten cents per million input tokens and fifty cents per million output tokens. That's cheap enough that teams can run high-volume jobs they couldn't justify before. Longer prompts cost five times as much, so the headline price only applies to shorter jobs. Haiku 5.5 is available now through Anthropic's API and the major cloud platforms.

It's also the first Haiku with adjustable effort controls, so developers can choose how hard the model thinks on each task. A simple sorting job can run fast and cheap, and a trickier request can get more reasoning. Asana staff software engineer Aaron Vinh reports that task completion time dropped by more than 30 percent when the company tested Haiku 5.5 in its AI Teammates product. Keep in mind that this is one customer's test, published by Anthropic, not independent validation.
